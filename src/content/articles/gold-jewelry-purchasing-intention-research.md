## 💍 What Really Makes People Buy Gold Jewelry?

> **"Factors Influencing Consumer Purchasing Intention Towards Gold Jewelry: A Survey with Reference to Kathmandu Valley"**

---

### 🎯 What it was about

Gold jewelry sits at a strange crossroads — part investment, part fashion, part emotional purchase. This study went straight to the consumers of Kathmandu Valley to figure out what *actually* tips someone into buying gold, testing five candidate psychological and practical drivers:

- **Buyer's Preference**
- **Self-Image**
- **Product Design**
- **Trust**
- **Expected Future Value**

---

### 🔍 How it was done

- **Primary data, straight from the source** — a structured questionnaire survey distributed online.
- **385 respondents** from Kathmandu Valley, spanning genders, age groups, and income brackets.
- Checked questionnaire reliability with **Cronbach's Alpha**.
- Ran **descriptive statistics**, **correlation analysis**, and a **multicollinearity check**.
- Built a **regression model** and formally tested five hypotheses — one for each proposed driver of purchasing intention.

---

### 🏆 What it found

- Respondents were **57% male, 43% female**, with nearly two-thirds aged **21–30**, and over a third earning above NPR 50,000/month.
- **3 out of 5 hypotheses held up:** **Buyer's Preference, Trust,** and **Expected Future Value** all showed a statistically significant, positive relationship with purchasing intention.
- **2 hypotheses were rejected:** **Self-Image** and **Product Design** showed no significant effect — surprising, given how much marketing leans on aesthetics and status appeal.
- **Trust in the jeweler and buying preference emerged as the single biggest influences** on the decision to buy.
- Practical recommendations followed directly from the data: build trust through better in-store service, go digital to meet a customer base that's shifting online, and lean into gold's role as a reliable store of future value — rather than over-investing in design-led marketing.

---

### 🧵 Bottom line

The "obvious" marketing instincts — flashy design, aspirational self-image — didn't hold up empirically. What actually drives gold jewelry purchases in Kathmandu Valley is far more grounded: trust and long-term financial value.
