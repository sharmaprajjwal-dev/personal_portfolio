## 🏦 Decoding Bank Health with the CAMEL Framework

> **"Analysis of Financial Performance of Commercial Banks in Nepal Based on the CAMEL Framework"**
> *A case study of Everest Bank, Himalayan Bank & Prime Commercial Bank*

---

### 🎯 What it was about

Nepal's economy runs on its banks — they hold the vast majority of the country's financial assets. But how do you actually tell if a bank is *healthy*? This study set out to answer that using **CAMEL** — the internationally recognized bank-health checkup:

| Letter | Stands for | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | Capital Adequacy | Can the bank absorb shocks and losses? |
| **A** | Asset Quality | How much of its loan book is going bad? |
| **M** | Management Efficiency | How well are costs controlled relative to income? |
| **E** | Earnings | How profitably is the bank deploying its money? |
| **L** | Liquidity | Can it meet withdrawal demands without a crisis? |

---

### 🔍 How it was done

- **5 years of hard data** (FY 2012/13 – 2016/17) pulled straight from the audited annual reports of the three banks.
- Calculated all five CAMEL ratios (CAR, NPL, MER, NIM, CDR) for every bank, every year.
- Ran **descriptive statistics** and **trend analysis** to rank the banks.
- Tested for **multicollinearity** to make sure the variables weren't secretly measuring the same thing.
- Used **correlation** and **multiple regression analysis** to test whether CAMEL ratios actually *predict* profitability — measured through **Return on Assets (ROA)** and **Return on Equity (ROE)**.

---

### 🏆 What it found

- **Everest Bank came out on top** — best composite CAMEL ranking, highest ROA (1.83%) and ROE (23.87%) — followed by Prime Bank, then Himalayan Bank.
- The regression models were strong: CAMEL variables explained **87.3% of the variation in ROA** and a striking **94.0% of the variation in ROE**.
- **Capital adequacy, bad loans, and management inefficiency all *dragged profitability down*** — the leaner and cleaner a bank's balance sheet, the better it performed.
- **Net interest margin was the strongest positive driver of both ROA and ROE** — banks that earned a healthy spread between what they paid and charged on money did better across the board.
- Liquidity (credit-to-deposit ratio) helped ROA, but didn't meaningfully move ROE.

---

### 🧵 Bottom line

The study didn't just rank three banks — it built statistical proof that the textbook CAMEL variables genuinely move the needle on real-world bank profitability in Nepal, and pinpointed exactly which levers matter most.
