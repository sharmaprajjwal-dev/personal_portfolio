## The Brief

The Pizza House Chitwan already had two assets many local restaurants struggle to build: a
recognisable wood-fired-pizza proposition and visible attention across Google Maps and social
media. The problem came after discovery. Customers did not have one dependable, business-led
destination where they could understand the offer, browse structured information, find the
location, evaluate group options, and move naturally towards an enquiry, order, call, or visit.

The brief was to close that gap without overengineering the solution or filling missing business
facts with plausible copy. The work needed to create an owned digital foundation, strengthen
local-search readiness, and make the customer journey measurable — while remaining maintainable
for a single-location restaurant with limited operating capacity.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Evidence and source audit**: reviewed the restaurant's supplied materials, Google Maps
  presence, social profiles, public reviews, competitors, and completed strategy research;
  separated confirmed facts from hypotheses and details requiring owner approval
- **Customer-journey design**: organised the experience around the decisions customers actually
  make — understanding the wood-fired offer, viewing menu information, checking location and
  access, considering group occasions, and choosing the next action
- **Local-search foundation**: connected location content, Google Maps, page metadata, internal
  links, structured data, sitemap, robots directives, and locally relevant supporting content
  into one coherent discovery system
- **Conversion-first website architecture**: built a responsive Astro and TypeScript site with
  reusable components, central business data, structured menu content, package collections, and
  clear mobile paths to contact, directions, and enquiry
- **Trust and accuracy controls**: designed safe states for unknown prices, missing imagery,
  unconfirmed contact details, unavailable items, and changing service information rather than
  publishing unsupported claims
- **Measurement and rollout planning**: defined the priority customer actions, tracking
  requirements, operational source-of-truth logic, and launch gates needed before paid
  acquisition could be treated as a serious growth channel

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- A complete responsive restaurant website with Home, Menu, Brunch Packages, About, Gallery,
  Blog, Contact, Privacy, and custom 404 pages
- A structured menu system supported by one maintainable data source, with safe handling for
  unknown prices, missing images, availability, and unconfirmed dietary information
- Reusable package and article collections designed for group occasions, local discovery, and
  future content growth without rebuilding the site architecture
- Clear customer pathways connecting offer information with Google Maps, contact, directions,
  and WhatsApp-ready enquiry fallbacks
- Technical SEO covering unique metadata, canonical URLs, sitemap, robots file, RSS,
  breadcrumbs, Restaurant schema, BlogPosting schema, and relevant FAQ markup
- An evidence-based Google Business Profile and local-discovery assessment covering profile
  completeness, menu presentation, reviews, customer actions, website alignment, and measurement
  readiness
- Conversion, analytics, content, and paid-search requirements tied together through a gated
  90-day implementation roadmap rather than a disconnected list of marketing tactics
- Plain-language content and confirmation guides so the owner can update business facts, menu
  data, packages, imagery, contact routes, and social links without changing the site's core code

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The central lesson was that this business did not need more disconnected channel activity. It
already had evidence of social reach and a credible local profile. What it lacked was a managed
bridge between discovery, decision support, customer action, restaurant follow-through, and
measurement. Treating the website, Google profile, content, conversion, and analytics as one
system produced a more useful strategy than optimising each platform in isolation.

The second lesson was operational. A local restaurant website can look complete while still
creating risk if its menu, prices, hours, services, and contact routes are guessed or become
outdated. Designing around verified facts and visible confirmation gaps made the result more
trustworthy and easier to maintain. For a business of this size, disciplined ownership and a
small number of reliable customer journeys matter more than technical complexity.
