## The Brief

Shaviv Nail Studio & Training Center is a mall-based nail studio and certified beauty-training
academy in Bharatpur, Chitwan — a dual-revenue business (retail services + vocational training)
run by a small, founder-led team. The brief was simple to state and hard to execute well:
step back from the day-to-day and produce a genuine, board-grade picture of where the business
stands and what to do next — the kind of diagnostic a Deloitte-style strategy team would hand
a board of directors, not a generic marketing audit.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The diagnostic was run as a structured, multi-lens strategic review rather than a single
framework applied once. Each lens was chosen to surface a different class of blind spot:

- **Market & competitive context** — industry overview, customer segmentation, and a
  named-competitor analysis benchmarking positioning, not just pricing
- **Structural analysis** — SWOT, PESTLE, Porter's Five Forces, VRIO, and a full Business
  Model Canvas, applied specifically to the business rather than described in the abstract
- **Operational health** — a business health scorecard, operational bottleneck review, and
  a value-chain breakdown across primary and support activities
- **Digital & AI maturity** — a capability-by-capability audit (booking, CRM, content,
  automation) benchmarked against what a business at this stage should already have in place
- **Risk and opportunity mapping** — a risk register and an impact-vs-effort opportunity
  matrix, used to force-rank what matters before anything gets prioritized
- **Synthesis** — every recommendation carries an explicit why / expected outcome /
  business value / implementation difficulty / priority, rolled into a 30/90/365-day
  action plan the board can actually execute against

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- A 25-section executive report, structured and written to board-presentation standard
- Full application (not just definitions) of ten-plus strategic frameworks to the
  business's actual position, competitors, and constraints
- A prioritized recommendation set, each scored on business value and implementation
  difficulty rather than presented as a flat list
- A phased 30/90/365-day action plan sequencing quick wins before capital-intensive moves
- A formatted, board-ready Word document, generated and visually verified end-to-end

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The advantage</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The value wasn't producing an opinion faster — it was replacing founder intuition with a
structured, defensible view the business could actually act on and revisit.

- **Sequencing over a wish list.** Every recommendation was force-ranked by impact against
  effort, so the business knows what to do first, not just what's possible
- **Board-literate output.** The report is written and structured the way a board actually
  reads — executive summary up front, evidence and frameworks in the body, one clear
  synthesis at the end — not a loose set of marketing tips
- **A model that scales with the business.** Because the diagnostic follows a repeatable
  methodology rather than a one-off narrative, the same structure can be re-run in 12 months
  to track what actually moved
- **Turnaround speed without losing rigor.** The full multi-framework diagnostic — research,
  analysis, drafting, and a formatted deliverable — was completed in a single engagement
  cycle, using AI-assisted research and drafting under senior-consultant direction rather
  than a multi-week agency timeline

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The most useful realization for a small, founder-run business is usually not a hidden
opportunity — it's permission to stop treating every idea as equally urgent. The real work
of a diagnostic like this is forcing a ranked order onto a list of things a founder already
half-knows, so that the next ninety days has a start point instead of a dozen.
