## The Brief

Following the initial business diagnostic, the second phase of the Shaviv engagement moved
from "what's true about the business" to "what do we do about it" — specifically, turning
the studio's existing social visibility into a repeatable booking and referral engine rather
than leaving that conversion gap unaddressed.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- Audited the existing content cadence and engagement patterns across Instagram, Facebook,
  and TikTok to identify where interest was being generated but not converted
- Mapped the current customer journey from discovery to booking to find the exact drop-off
  point between social interest and a completed appointment
- Designed a lightweight, low-cost funnel (booking link, lead capture, referral incentive)
  sized appropriately for a founder-run team, not an enterprise marketing stack
- Sequenced the rollout so quick wins (booking link, referral offer) landed before
  higher-effort work (content calendar discipline, repositioning messaging)

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- A booking-and-lead-capture rollout plan across all three social channels
- A structured weekly content cadence template, sized to the team's actual capacity
- A referral-incentive structure for both studio clients and academy alumni
- A simple before/after tracking sheet to measure booking conversion over the following
  90 days

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The advantage</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **No new spend required.** The plan converts existing reach rather than asking the
  business to buy more of it
- **Matched to team capacity.** The cadence and funnel were sized to what a small,
  founder-led team could realistically sustain, not an idealized marketing calendar
- **Measurable from day one.** A simple tracking sheet means the business can see whether
  the funnel is working within weeks, not quarters

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

Growth work for a business this size rarely fails from a lack of ideas — it fails when the
plan assumes more time or headcount than the team actually has. The most useful part of this
phase was cutting the plan down to what could genuinely get done in a normal week.

</div>
</details>
