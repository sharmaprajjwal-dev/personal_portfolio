## The Brief

The social audit showed that The Pizza House could attract attention, particularly through
short-form video, but did not yet have a repeatable system for turning that attention into local
demand and trust. Publishing needed to become easier to sustain, more consistent across channels,
and more closely connected to customer questions and business actions.

The brief was to build an operating plan the team could actually manage — not a high-volume
calendar detached from restaurant service, approvals, assets, or measurement.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Audience and journey translation**: converted the four customer archetypes and journey stages
  into content needs, proof requirements, and action prompts
- **Platform-role design**: assigned TikTok to appetite and reach, Instagram to curated visual
  consideration, and Facebook to practical information and community utility
- **Brand communication system**: defined positioning, message hierarchy, tone of voice, and the
  balance between wood-fired differentiation and group-friendly breadth
- **Content operating model**: created pillars, format rules, weekly capture, repurposing,
  publishing cadence, approval, response, and escalation workflows
- **Governance and measurement**: connected content decisions to ownership, customer service,
  privacy, AI review, meaningful KPIs, and a recurring management rhythm

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- Strategic content objectives, priority audience framework, and platform strategy
- Brand communication principles, tone of voice, five content pillars, content mix, and
  customer-journey content map
- Short-form video, Stories, repurposing, and format strategies supported by a reusable idea bank
- A recommended publishing cadence, weekly structure, and 30-day sample content calendar
- Caption, keyword, hashtag, community-management, response, reputation, user-generated-content,
  partnership, and campaign frameworks
- Production workflow, role definitions, approval governance, AI-assisted drafting controls,
  capacity plan, measurement framework, testing approach, and risk register

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The result</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Social activity became a manageable operating system** rather than a collection of isolated
  post ideas
- **Five recurring content jobs created balance** across appetite, social occasions, practical
  decision support, trust, advocacy, and genuine offers
- **One weekly capture model reduced production pressure** by creating reusable vertical video,
  still imagery, Stories, and platform-specific adaptations from the same source material
- **Governance protected execution quality** through fact approval, customer consent, response
  ownership, escalation, and mandatory human review of AI-assisted output

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The strongest content plan was not the one with the most posts. It was the one the restaurant
could sustain while preserving accurate information, authentic imagery, responsive community
management, and service capacity.

Existing social reach created permission to focus on quality and conversion rather than chasing
volume. The next level of maturity depended on connecting content to customer decisions and
business outcomes while keeping the production process simple enough to survive busy operating
weeks.
