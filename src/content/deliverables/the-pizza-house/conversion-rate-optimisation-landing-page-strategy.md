## The Brief

The restaurant's strongest digital assets sat at the top of the journey: social attention, local
recognition, reviews, and a clear product story. The weaker point was the hand-off. Customers had
to assemble menu, price, location, proof, fulfilment, and action information across platforms,
with limited visibility into completion or confirmation.

The brief was to define the highest-value conversions, diagnose the friction affecting each
customer journey, and create a practical landing-page and testing strategy without defaulting to
a complete redesign.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Conversion-objective definition**: separated completed or confirmed business outcomes from
  supporting clicks, page interactions, and other diagnostic events
- **Journey and funnel mapping**: examined visit, call, order, takeaway, delivery, group enquiry,
  and research pathways across Google, social, website, messaging, and external hand-offs
- **Friction and objection analysis**: assessed uncertainty around menu, price, suitability,
  trust, location, fulfilment, response, and what happens after the customer acts
- **Landing-page architecture**: defined message match, page portfolio, value proposition,
  information hierarchy, proof, CTA, mobile, accessibility, and confirmation requirements
- **Testing and governance design**: created a research backlog, prioritisation logic, tracking
  needs, reporting rhythm, ownership model, and ethical testing controls

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- Executive conversion assessment, business objectives, funnel definitions, and priority journeys
- Friction, objection, value-proposition, and message-match strategies
- A landing-page portfolio and blueprint covering pizza/menu, delivery and takeaway, visit/location,
  and group or occasion-led needs
- Homepage, product/service, local-page, CTA, form, booking, checkout, call, trust, review,
  pricing, navigation, mobile, accessibility, speed, and confirmation recommendations
- Lead-handling and operational-conversion requirements connecting digital actions to restaurant
  response and fulfilment
- CRO research, experiment prioritisation, backlog, measurement, tracking, governance, risk, and
  phased implementation frameworks

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The result</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Four priority customer paths replaced a generic website objective**, giving each major intent
  a clear information and action requirement
- **One action hierarchy created consistency** across menu, order or call, and directions without
  forcing every page to carry competing CTAs
- **Trust moved closer to the decision**, with reviews, practical facts, fulfilment clarity, and
  confirmation treated as conversion components rather than supporting decoration
- **Acquisition and conversion became one decision system**: paid traffic should wait until the
  relevant page, primary action, tracking, and operational follow-through are ready

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The main conversion problem was structural rather than cosmetic. Changing button colours would
not solve a journey in which customers still had to search across platforms for the information
needed to make a confident choice.

For this restaurant, good CRO meant reducing uncertainty and hand-offs before pursuing
sophisticated experimentation. One verified source of truth, clear intent-led pages, a primary
action, and real confirmation provide a stronger foundation than a large test backlog without
reliable outcomes.
