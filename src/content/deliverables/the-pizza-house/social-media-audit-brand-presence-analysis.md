## The Brief

The restaurant was active across three social platforms, but activity was uneven. TikTok showed
the strongest awareness potential, Facebook carried the clearest practical information, and
Instagram remained comparatively small. The brand and location were recognisable, yet profile
links, messages, proof, and calls to action did not create one dependable route from attention to
customer action.

The brief was to assess whether the social ecosystem supported discovery, trust, decision-making,
community interaction, and conversion — not simply whether content was being posted.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Platform-role assessment**: evaluated strategic fit, audience behaviour, business purpose,
  and likely contribution of TikTok, Facebook, and Instagram
- **Profile and brand audit**: reviewed names, biographies, links, contact information, visual
  identity, positioning, and cross-platform consistency
- **Representative content analysis**: assessed formats, subjects, creative quality, captions,
  discoverability signals, posting cadence, and video execution
- **Engagement and journey review**: examined comments, customer-service signals, calls to action,
  hand-offs to Google or website destinations, and movement across journey stages
- **Competitor and maturity benchmark**: compared visible execution with relevant local businesses
  and assessed readiness across content, community, conversion, and measurement

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- An executive assessment of the social ecosystem and the distinct role of each active platform
- Profile-completeness, brand-consistency, value-proposition, content-portfolio, caption, keyword,
  visual, and short-form-video audits
- Posting-cadence, engagement, community-interaction, sentiment, and social customer-service
  assessments
- Persona and customer-journey alignment across discovery, consideration, action, experience,
  and advocacy
- CTA and conversion-path analysis covering profile links, practical information, and the hand-off
  to owned or local-search destinations
- Competitor benchmark, strengths, risk and opportunity register, maturity assessment, and
  measurement-readiness review

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The result</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **TikTok was confirmed as the primary awareness asset**, with evidence that selected content
  could travel beyond the existing follower base
- **Facebook's role became clearer** as the practical information and community-support channel,
  while Instagram required a stronger visual and proof system
- **The central problem shifted from posting volume to hand-off quality** — attention was not
  consistently connected to menu, location, contact, order, or measurable action
- **Leadership gained channel discipline**: protect authentic social strengths, correct profile
  routes, and evaluate content by its customer job rather than vanity metrics alone

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The most visible social metric was not the most important business finding. An outlier video can
prove creative distribution potential, but it cannot show whether the restaurant gained a visit,
order, or repeat customer.

The real opportunity was to assign each platform a clear role and connect social proof to the
next decision. That requires fewer disconnected publishing decisions, more accurate destinations,
and a content system designed around appetite, occasions, practical confidence, trust, and action.

</div>
</details>
