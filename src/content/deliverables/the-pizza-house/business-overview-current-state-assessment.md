## The Brief

The Pizza House Chitwan had a recognisable public presence, a wood-fired-pizza proposition,
and several ways for customers to interact with the restaurant. What leadership did not have
was one reliable assessment of how the business actually worked: who it served, which occasions
it competed for, how its broad menu supported the offer, where digital activity contributed,
and which constraints would affect growth.

The brief was to establish that baseline before recommending channels, campaigns, technology,
or investment. The assessment had to separate confirmed public evidence from reasonable
hypotheses and avoid turning incomplete information into false certainty.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Business evidence review**: consolidated supplied business information, public profiles,
  menu evidence, location details, reviews, and observable customer pathways
- **Business-model assessment**: examined dine-in, takeaway, delivery, direct ordering, group
  occasions, menu breadth, cross-selling potential, and operational complexity
- **Value-proposition analysis**: separated the lead wood-fired-pizza identity from the broader
  group-friendly menu and mall-location advantages
- **Customer and journey framing**: identified the likely decision-makers, occasions, needs,
  trust requirements, and points of friction shaping restaurant choice
- **Digital maturity review**: assessed how Google, TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, messaging, and
  the available website presence worked together — and where the hand-offs broke down

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- An executive business snapshot covering offer, location, operating model, customer actions,
  apparent positioning, and current digital presence
- A clear articulation of the customer value proposition and the evidence supporting it
- Priority customer groups and occasions, including peer gatherings, social dining, family
  coordination, mall visits, takeaway, delivery, and group enquiries
- A business-model assessment covering revenue sources, transaction structure, repeat-purchase
  considerations, operational complexity, and scalability constraints
- A current-state synthesis distinguishing genuine strengths, material gaps, and evidence that
  still required management validation

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The result</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **One shared business baseline** replaced disconnected assumptions about the restaurant,
  customer, offer, and digital channels
- **The lead position became clearer**: wood-fired pizza provides memorability, while menu
  breadth, mall access, and multiple fulfilment options support group utility
- **The central digital constraint was identified early** — the business was more mature at
  attracting attention than at creating a controlled, measurable path to customer action
- **Later analysis gained a stable foundation** for market, competitor, customer, conversion,
  acquisition, measurement, and implementation decisions

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The most useful outcome was not a long description of the restaurant. It was a disciplined
definition of what could be treated as fact, what was strategically important, and what still
needed validation. That prevented later recommendations from being built on invented prices,
capacity, customer behaviour, or financial performance.

For a small local business, the current-state assessment also exposed how closely marketing and
operations are connected. A strong proposition creates potential, but accurate information,
service consistency, response ownership, and fulfilment determine whether that potential becomes
a real customer outcome.
