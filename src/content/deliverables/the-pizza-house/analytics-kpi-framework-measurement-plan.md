## The Brief

The restaurant could observe attention and actions across social platforms, Google, messaging,
and website destinations, but those signals did not consistently connect to confirmed calls,
orders, visits, enquiries, revenue, or repeat behaviour. Leadership therefore lacked a reliable
basis for comparing channel quality or deciding where additional investment belonged.

The brief was to create a measurement system small enough for the business to operate and strong
enough to support real decisions — without confusing more tools or dashboard cards with better
evidence.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Business-objective translation**: connected acquisition, conversion, customer experience,
  retention, operational responsiveness, and efficiency to measurable decisions
- **KPI and conversion hierarchy**: separated confirmed business outcomes, primary conversions,
  secondary actions, leading indicators, and diagnostic engagement metrics
- **Journey and channel measurement**: mapped website, local search, Google Business Profile,
  social, content, paid media, CRO, calls, and third-party hand-offs
- **Technical architecture design**: defined GA4, Google Tag Manager, event naming, UTMs,
  attribution, call tracking, offline outcomes, CRM considerations, and cross-domain needs
- **Governance and quality controls**: established sources of truth, dashboards, reporting,
  target-setting, QA, privacy, access, documentation, roles, alerts, and maturity phases

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- Business-objective framework, KPI hierarchy, leading and lagging indicators, and conversion
  definitions
- Customer-journey, funnel, channel, website, SEO, Google profile, social, paid-media, CRO,
  retention, and operational measurement frameworks
- Metric dictionary, event-tracking plan, naming governance, GA4 plan, Tag Manager plan, and
  conversion architecture
- UTM, attribution, call-tracking, offline-conversion, CRM, and third-party measurement guidance
- Source-of-truth hierarchy and four dashboard views for executive, channel, operational, and
  diagnostic decisions
- Reporting cadence, insight process, target-setting rules, data-quality and QA framework,
  privacy, ownership, documentation, governance, maturity assessment, gap register, and phased plan

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The result</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Measurement maturity was assessed as Emerging**, preventing platform activity from being
  presented as proven commercial performance
- **A clear source-of-truth hierarchy was established**: confirmed operational outcomes first,
  customer actions second, and reach or engagement as diagnostic signals
- **The system was deliberately constrained** to a focused objective set, KPI levels, core events,
  and decision views rather than an oversized reporting environment
- **Leadership gained rules for responsible target-setting**: baselines, data quality, operating
  capacity, and reconciliation must exist before efficiency or growth targets are approved

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The most important analytics decision was conceptual: a click is not an order, a profile view is
not a visit, and video reach is not revenue. Once that hierarchy was explicit, the technical plan
became much simpler.

For a business of this size, reliable operational capture mattered more than sophisticated
attribution. A small outcome log adopted by staff can provide more decision value than an advanced
dashboard built on incomplete or unverified events.
