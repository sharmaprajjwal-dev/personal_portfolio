The audit used publicly visible profile information, representative review and image samples,

## The Brief

Google was already one of the restaurant's most commercially important surfaces. The profile
helped exact-brand customers find the location, read reviews, request directions, and move towards
an order. The question was whether it also gave a new customer enough accurate information and
confidence to choose the business — and whether it competed effectively when the search was not
for the restaurant's name.

The brief was to audit the profile as a discovery and conversion system, not merely check whether
a listing existed.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Profile completeness review**: assessed business name, address, contact routes, hours,
  description, categories, attributes, accessibility, menu, website, ordering, and booking fields
- **Customer-action testing**: reviewed the paths to call, directions, menu, website, order,
  products, services, and other supported actions on mobile-led journeys
- **Review and response analysis**: sampled recent, positive, negative, and mixed reviews to
  identify recurring customer themes and the quality of owner responses
- **Photo, post, and Q&A review**: inspected representative owner and customer imagery, visible
  updates, common questions, and information gaps without over-sampling
- **Local visibility and competitor benchmark**: tested a focused set of branded and non-branded
  themes and compared profile maturity with relevant local competitors

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- An executive profile assessment covering accuracy, completeness, discovery readiness,
  reputation, customer actions, website alignment, competitiveness, and measurement maturity
- A field-level completeness audit and business-category assessment
- Products, services, business-description, review-theme, response-quality, photo, posts, and Q&A
  evaluations
- Customer-action pathways for calls, directions, menu access, website visits, ordering, and
  other supported actions
- Local-information consistency checks across the profile, website, social channels, and public
  platforms
- A focused local-search visibility review, competitor benchmark, risk and opportunity register,
  maturity assessment, and measurement-readiness diagnosis

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The result</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **The profile was assessed as a credible but incomplete local asset**, with exact-brand
  discoverability, a meaningful review base, and useful service and accessibility attributes
- **The highest-impact gaps became explicit**: website connection, structured menu presentation,
  non-branded visibility, response consistency, and reliable action measurement
- **Reputation was connected back to operations** because public praise and criticism showed that
  service and fulfilment consistency affected the value of the profile
- **Leadership received a prioritised local-discovery agenda** without overstating the direct
  ranking impact of posts, photos, or any single profile field

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The profile was not simply another directory listing. For many local customers it functioned as
the practical homepage: the place where they evaluated reviews, checked the location, and decided
whether to call, visit, or continue researching.

The audit also showed why local SEO and customer experience cannot be separated. Better profile
completeness can improve confidence, but only consistent food, service, fulfilment, and review
handling can sustain the reputation signals customers rely on.

</details>
