The roadmap uses relative effort, role types, and decision gates because staff capacity, vendor

## The Brief

The completed analysis produced a large body of findings across business strategy, customers,
competitors, website experience, local discovery, social media, content, paid acquisition,
conversion, analytics, and automation. Leadership needed those findings translated into one
manageable programme rather than a collection of parallel recommendations.

The brief was to define what should happen first, what depended on earlier work, who should own
each activity, which decisions required leadership approval, and what evidence had to exist before
the business increased acquisition activity.

<div class="cs-accordion not-prose">

<details open>
<summary>Methodology</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **Portfolio prioritisation**: evaluated initiatives by business and customer impact, urgency,
  confidence, effort, dependency, and risk
- **Three-phase sequencing**: organised the programme into foundation and control, build and
  activation readiness, then controlled launch and learning
- **Dependency mapping**: linked account access, approved facts, pages, tracking, customer
  response, content assets, service capacity, privacy, and budget approvals
- **Ownership and governance design**: assigned accountable and responsible role types, weekly
  management rhythm, escalation rules, and decision gates
- **Evidence-based completion logic**: defined deliverables, validation methods, milestone proof,
  and continue, revise, pause, or scale decisions

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What was delivered</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- A complete 90-day programme across three phases and 13 implementation weeks
- Prioritisation method, strategic outcomes, phase actions, weekly schedule, and workstream view
- Quick wins covering links, profile destinations, action hierarchy, menu ownership, responses,
  outcome logging, and real-device journey testing
- Critical dependencies, role and ownership model, relative resource plan, and eight decision
  gates from mobilisation through the Day-90 investment review
- KPI and milestone framework, implementation risk register, change-management approach, and
  weekly decision rhythm
- Formal 30-, 60-, and 90-day reviews plus next-quarter handover requirements

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>The result</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

- **The strategy became executable** through a defined sequence of owners, dependencies,
  deliverables, deadlines, and evidence checks
- **Days 1–30 protected the programme** by prioritising access, approved facts, customer response,
  conversion definitions, tracking QA, and operational outcome capture
- **Days 31–60 built the customer and marketing foundation** through priority pages, local
  alignment, content operations, campaign preparation, and reporting
- **Days 61–90 permitted only controlled activation**, with paid Search dependent on readiness and
  the final decision based on reconciled outcomes rather than platform activity alone

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reflection</summary>
<div class="cs-panel">

The main execution risk was not a lack of ideas. It was allowing visible growth activity to move
faster than ownership, measurement, customer response, or service capacity. The roadmap therefore
treated sequencing as a control rather than a planning preference.

Day-90 success was deliberately defined as a functioning management system and an initial
baseline, not guaranteed sales growth. That standard created a more honest foundation for the
next-quarter decision: expand, maintain, refine, or stop based on evidence.

</div>
</details>
